Home

The screen opened fifty times a day. It answers what a contractor cannot answer from memory: how the money stands, whether the month is on pace, what the last quarter actually grossed, and where the work went.

The frame — header, rail, dock, timer bar — is _shell.html. This page specifies the content column, which is the panel.

What earns a place

Toggl's dashboard answers how did I spend my time? For a solo contractor that is the wrong question — you already know, you were there.

  • A region ships only if it carries a number the user cannot compute in their head, or a row they can click to act on. Preferably both. "Interesting" is not the bar.
  • Stats are denominated in money. Hours are the raw material; dollars are what the user thinks in. Unbillable work has no rate by definition, so those rows show hours and an em-dash.
  • GET /stats is one call. The regions render together, and a set that pops in piecemeal reads as broken.
  • Nothing here writes. Every region reads, and every action on one is a link to the surface that owns the mutation — a dashboard that edits data turns a stray click into a changed invoice.
  • There is one view. No hiding a region, no reordering them. "Pick your own dashboard" is the most natural path by which a restraint-first product becomes the thing it was built against.
One surface, three rows

The content column is a single borderless panel on the ground. The regions are parts of it, not cards on it: nothing inside the panel carries a border, a background or a shadow of its own. Rows of regions separate by an inset rule with equal space above and below.

The panel is wide enough for two columns, and the regions pair by subject: a figure beside the breakdown that explains it. Money collected sits beside money still owed; the month sits beside the quarter. The year spans both, because a 52-week heatmap at half width is a texture nobody can read.

    Three rows. The rules are inset and horizontal; nothing inside has an edge of its own
    • Row order is fixed: where the money stands, then whether the month is working, then where the year went. No hiding a region, no reordering them.
    • The columns size by @container, not viewport. The panel is not the window: the rail and the dock claim their space at lg and xl, so the panel is 780px at a 1100px window and 732px at 1440 — wider at the narrower window. A viewport breakpoint would collapse the wide one and split the narrow one.
    • Below the threshold the pairs stack, in the order they read: Collected, Owed, the month, Velocity, the year.
    • The left column is the wider, at 1.15fr to 1fr. It carries the figures, and an equal split leaves client names truncating while the money column has room to spare.
    • Collected never hides, because an account that has collected nothing renders $0.00 — true, and the figure its first payment moves. Owed hides with nothing awaiting and nothing unbilled: with neither figure there is no list either, and a region with no rows is a heading over a gap.
    • Rows size by @container too. The same row renders in this panel and in the narrow dock, where a sm: breakpoint is true at 1600px and lays out as though there were room.

    Separation is space and an inset horizontal rule, nothing else.

    • Never a vertical rule between the columns. It rebuilds the gridlines this panel exists without, and reads the pair back as two cards.
    • Never a full-bleed horizontal rule. Inset to the rows' own 16px; edge to edge it cuts the panel in two and reads as two stacked cards.
    The region header, in two modes

    • The icon is aria-hidden. It is a second channel for a region you are scanning, not part of its name — without it a screen reader announces "banknote Collected".

    The month

    A cumulative line of accumulated hours — or money, following the goal's unit — against the goal ray. The gap between the two lines is the delta, rendered as a distance rather than a percentage to decode.

    No target — the region stays, carrying the way in
    • The ray steps on business days only. A ray sloping through the weekend shows the user behind every Saturday and recovered every Monday, which is the noise the business-day rule exists to kill.
    • The actual line stops at today and is null beyond it. A line drawn flat to the 31st reads as a month that stopped working, not a month still in progress.
    • Weekend work is carried onto the next business day's point, never discarded, so the cumulative total always equals the month's actual.
    • The target is context, not part of the figure — set at row scale beside it rather than carried along at 30px.
    • With no target the region stays, carrying a line that names what a goal is for and a link to the field. Hiding it made the feature invisible to the only account that had never set one.
    • The scale is the target, unless the month ran past it — a line that leaves the top of the box would misreport a month that beat its goal.
    • Settings owns the goal field. The pencil is a link, never a dialog: a second editor here is a second place to look when the number is wrong.
    Velocity

    Gross over the trailing three months, per month, and the mix that made it up. It sits beside the month plot, and it is a different object from Owed: a figure, a bar, and a key.

    • The mix is a bar, never a row list. Owed already carries per-client rows. A second region in that shape — same columns, same trailing arrow, and with a full book the same order of magnitude — is read at a glance as the first one printed twice.
    • The bar is the region's picture, one segment per client, sized by that client's share of the gross.
    • The segments are muted. At full strength the client hues across the panel's width pull harder than the running timer, which is the one thing on screen allowed to shout.
    • The key is inline text with a swatch — a name and a figure. No aging column, no amount column, no arrow: those are the row shape this region exists not to be.
    • The figure is per month, with /mo gross beside it. The window total carries no rate, so two windows of different lengths cannot be compared by it.
    • "Gross earned", never "earned" alone. It is work done over the window, not money collected, and /mo gross is what says so.
    • The invoiced/unbilled split is not printed. Its figures are window totals while the headline is per month, so they do not reconcile with the number above them, and the unbilled half restates the figure Owed already carries. The gap still reads when it matters: the unbilled figure exceeding this window's gross is the signal.
    • The headline takes the sent beat, alone, and neutrally. Raising an invoice moves money across the split without changing the gross, so the figure does not move — the motion marks the event, and it leaves with the tween.
    • Not comparable with awaiting payment, which spans every period rather than this window.
    • The hours sit alone under the bar, right-aligned. The one figure in the region that is not money.
    • A client's share is its gross, invoiced plus unbilled. A client whose whole window is still unbilled grosses its unbilled amount, not nothing.
    The year

    Fifty-two weeks, one cell per day. Colour is the client, density is the hours. A year is the window where a seasonal shape — a quiet August, a client that ended in March — becomes visible at all.

    • Hue is the client, resolved as everywhere else, and never the accent — that belongs to the running timer. Internal work keeps a neutral that reads as worked rather than as rest.
    • A blank day stays blank. Every day in the year gets a cell, so a gap is a weekend or a dry spell rather than missing data.
    • A day split across clients takes the one with the most hours. A cell is about 11px and cannot carry a stack; the alternative is a smear of colour that names nobody.
    • Archived clients keep their colour. Work billed to a finished engagement belongs in the history, and dropping the hue would silently reassign those hours to the neutral band.
    • The busiest day sets the density scale, floored so the lightest worked day is still visibly a day worked.
    • The streak sits in the header at row scale. It survives one missed day and breaks on two — a streak that breaks on a single missed day punishes one appointment and stops being a figure anyone trusts. Today not yet worked does not break it.
    • Each cell's date and hours are in its tooltip. There is no axis; at 52 columns every label is noise.
    The beats

    Three events move a figure rather than replacing it, and each is reported under the figure it moved. The screen's largest numbers change for reasons the user cannot compute in their head — a four-level rate lookup, an invoice clearing — and landing on the new figure without the travel makes the old one look wrong.

    EventReported under
    stopThe owed figures
    sentThe owed figures, and Velocity's headline
    paidThe collected figure
    • A stop is reported where the work landed. Work just finished is unbilled work, so the beat sits under the owed figures; the amount is the resolved one, which is the number the user cannot arrive at alone.
    • Today's total outranks the stop chip, in the same slot. Three stops at $40, $30 and $80 read +$150 today: it is a fact about the day rather than a flash about a fetch, so it survives a refresh. The chip takes the slot only for the one thing the total cannot say — an unbillable stop earned no money, and +$0.00 today would teach the user that only billable work makes the app react.
    • An unbillable stop moves the hours and says so. It reports what happened rather than praising it.
    • The stop beat is neutral, never green. The accent is the running timer, and a stop has just ended one — borrowing it marks as live the one thing that stopped being live.
    • A send moves a boundary, not a total. Unbilled falls by exactly what awaiting gains, and Velocity's headline marks the event without moving: nothing was earned, and a figure that grew when an invoice was raised would be the screen inventing money.
    • A payment is reported under Collected, which is the figure it moves. It does not touch unbilled at all — that money left when the invoice was raised — so a countdown there would report a loss at the moment the user got paid.
    • The paid beat is the one use of the success green here, and it leaves with the tween. A stop and a send are things the user just did; a payment is the one outcome that happened to them, and spending the colour on all three spends it on nothing.
    • One refetch reports one event. Two at once resolve to no beat: each amount would carry the other's movement mixed in, and a figure that is the net of two unrelated events is money the app invented.
    • A figure arrives near its value, never from zero. A full climb animates the user's whole history as though it had just happened, and a frame far below the figure is a balance they do not have.
    • Reduced motion renders the settled figure — not a jump to zero and not a skipped render. The number is the content, so suppressing the animation must leave the answer on screen.
    • An animation never leaves a figure showing anything but the server's value. The tween ends on the target itself, never on its last interpolation: a billing screen that settles a cent off has silently changed what it reports.
    • The regions still never mutate. The beats are reactions to mutations owned by other screens, read off /stats — which every one of them already invalidates.
    Constraints this screen inherits
    • The accent appears nowhere on this screen. It is spent on the running timer in the bar below. success is the accent a step down, and it carries the collected plot and the paid beat — an outcome that has already happened, never a live thing.
    • Warning is the only alert channel, and the month's delta is the only thing on this screen that uses it.
    • Durations and currency are mono and tabular, without exception. A money column that reflows as digits change is the same defect as a timer that does.
    • Aggregate rate resolution belongs in SQL. resolve_entry_rate(uuid) is per-entry; Owed and Velocity sum across hundreds of entries and must not call it N times. A set-returning rollup is the correct shape.
    • 0 is a valid rate. Null-coalescing, never truthiness.
    Rejected — each will look appealing again
    • Hiding or reordering regions. There is one view. A dashboard the user assembles is a dashboard nobody can specify, and it is the path by which this becomes the product it was built against.
    • A range control on the year. The window is fixed at 52 weeks. A picker turns one honest shape into four that disagree, and each switch refetches a year.
    • Stacking a split day's clients in one cell. At 11px a stack is a smear. The majority takes the cell.
    • Top projects this week. With one to three clients, a ranked table ranks one thing.
    • Pie charts. A solo contractor's time splits two to four ways. A bar does that in less space and stays readable when one slice is 4%.
    • Team and comparison stats. There is no team. This is the slot Toggl wastes.
    What this screen takes from the API
    CallFeedsNote
    GET /stats Collected, Owed, the month, Velocity Collected per month with its trailing total, unbilled by client with aging, what is awaiting and across how many invoices, the month's cumulative series against its ray, trailing-quarter gross by client, and the billable ratio. One request, because they render together.
    GET /calendar?granularity=day the year One call over a one-year range. Already groups by local day server-side, which is the DST-correct bucketing this needs and the reason not to build a second one. Returns { date, totalSeconds, byClient } and nothing else.
    GET /clients?includeArchived the year's hues and legend Archived clients included, so a finished engagement keeps its colour instead of falling into the neutral band.

    The year's cache key is its own, not a variant of the activity key: the range is fixed, so sharing one with a range the user picks would refetch 365 days on every change. It joins invalidateEntryData(), or the region goes stale on every timer stop.

    The target lives on user_settings as monthly_target and monthly_target_unit, paired by a check constraint so neither can be set without the other — so the Settings field writes both in one patch, and clearing one clears both. Revenue's figure comes from month_revenue(), whose coalesce chain must stay identical to the other three — see data-model.md.

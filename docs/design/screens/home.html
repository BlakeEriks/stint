Home

The screen opened fifty times a day. It answers what a contractor cannot answer from memory: how much money is sitting unbilled, whether the month is on pace, what the last quarter actually grossed, and where the work went.

The frame — header, rail, dock, timer bar — is _shell.html. This page specifies the content column, which is the panel.

What earns a place

Toggl's dashboard answers how did I spend my time? For a solo contractor that is the wrong question — you already know, you were there.

  • A region ships only if it carries a number the user cannot compute in their head, or a row they can click to act on. Preferably both. "Interesting" is not the bar.
  • Stats are denominated in money. Hours are the raw material; dollars are what the user thinks in. Unbillable work has no rate by definition, so those rows show hours and an em-dash.
  • GET /stats is one call. The regions render together, and a set that pops in piecemeal reads as broken.
  • Nothing here writes. Every region reads, and every action on one is a link to the surface that owns the mutation — a dashboard that edits data turns a stray click into a changed invoice.
  • There is one view. No hiding a region, no reordering them. "Pick your own dashboard" is the most natural path by which a restraint-first product becomes the thing it was built against.
One surface, four regions

The content column is a single borderless panel on the ground. The four regions are parts of it, not cards on it: nothing inside the panel carries a border, a background or a shadow of its own. Regions separate by an inset rule with equal space above and below.

One panel. The rules are inset; nothing inside has an edge of its own
  • Region order is fixed: Unbilled, the month, the trailing quarter, the year. Money waiting, then whether the month is working, then what the work has been worth, then where it went.
  • Unbilled hides itself with nothing outstanding and takes its rule with it. A region with no rows is a heading over a gap.
  • The rule is inset to the rows' own 16px, never a border on a header. Full-bleed it cuts the panel in two and reads as two stacked cards, which is the shape this screen stopped using.
  • Rows size by @container, not viewport. The same row renders in this panel and in the narrow dock, where a sm: breakpoint is true at 1600px and lays out as though there were room.

  • The icon is aria-hidden. It is a second channel for a region you are scanning, not part of its name — without it a screen reader announces "wallet Unbilled".
  • The icon is neutral unless the region is about something being wrong, and never the accent.
  • At most one quiet control, top-right against the title. The month's pencil is the only one.
Unbilled

The headline number, and the reason this screen exists. Work done and not yet invoiced — money the user might still never see.

  • Never labelled "earned" or "revenue". Overstating it in a billing tool is the same trust failure as silently editing an entry.
  • Each row links to invoice generation for that client, which is what makes the region an action rather than a readout.
  • Work with no resolvable rate shows an em-dash, not $0.00. A zero looks like a real figure and understates the total; unratedCount says the total is incomplete rather than low.
  • Awaiting payment is never added to the total. That is work not yet invoiced; this is money already asked for, and summing them double-counts the same hours.
  • Past five clients the remainder is a count, the same shape the year's legend uses.
The month

A cumulative line of accumulated hours — or money, following the goal's unit — against the goal ray. The gap between the two lines is the delta, rendered as a distance rather than a percentage to decode.

No target — the region stays, carrying the way in
  • The ray steps on business days only. A ray sloping through the weekend shows the user behind every Saturday and recovered every Monday, which is the noise the business-day rule exists to kill.
  • The actual line stops at today and is null beyond it. A line drawn flat to the 31st reads as a month that stopped working, not a month still in progress.
  • Weekend work is carried onto the next business day's point, never discarded, so the cumulative total always equals the month's actual.
  • The target is context, not part of the figure — set at row scale beside it rather than carried along at 30px.
  • With no target the region stays, carrying a line that names what a goal is for and a link to the field. Hiding it made the feature invisible to the only account that had never set one.
  • The scale is the target, unless the month ran past it — a line that leaves the top of the box would misreport a month that beat its goal.
  • Settings owns the goal field. The pencil is a link, never a dialog: a second editor here is a second place to look when the number is wrong.
Velocity

Gross over the trailing three months, split by where the work stands now, then broken down by client.

  • "Gross earned", never "earned" alone. It is work done over the window, not money collected, and the invoiced/unbilled split is what says so.
  • invoiced + unbilled is the total. The split moves as invoices are raised while the total does not — the bar shows one number in two parts, never two numbers to add.
  • Not comparable with awaiting payment, which spans every period rather than this window.
  • Each client row is the client's gross, with its unbilled share as the detail. A client whose whole window is still unbilled grosses its unbilled amount, not nothing.
  • Rows link to the client, which is where rates and projects are changed.
The year

Fifty-two weeks, one cell per day. Colour is the client, density is the hours. A year is the window where a seasonal shape — a quiet August, a client that ended in March — becomes visible at all.

  • Hue is the client, resolved as everywhere else, and never the accent — that belongs to the running timer. Internal work keeps a neutral that reads as worked rather than as rest.
  • A blank day stays blank. Every day in the year gets a cell, so a gap is a weekend or a dry spell rather than missing data.
  • A day split across clients takes the one with the most hours. A cell is about 11px and cannot carry a stack; the alternative is a smear of colour that names nobody.
  • Archived clients keep their colour. Work billed to a finished engagement belongs in the history, and dropping the hue would silently reassign those hours to the neutral band.
  • The busiest day sets the density scale, floored so the lightest worked day is still visibly a day worked.
  • The streak sits in the header at row scale. It survives one missed day and breaks on two — a streak that breaks on a single missed day punishes one appointment and stops being a figure anyone trusts. Today not yet worked does not break it.
  • Each cell's date and hours are in its tooltip. There is no axis; at 52 columns every label is noise.
Constraints this screen inherits
  • Nothing here is green. The accent is spent on the running timer in the bar below.
  • Warning is the only alert channel, and the month's delta is the only thing on this screen that uses it. Success cyan appears nowhere — nothing here is an outcome.
  • Durations and currency are mono and tabular, without exception. An unbilled column that reflows as digits change is the same defect as a timer that does.
  • Aggregate rate resolution belongs in SQL. resolve_entry_rate(uuid) is per-entry; Unbilled and Velocity sum across hundreds of entries and must not call it N times. A set-returning rollup is the correct shape.
  • 0 is a valid rate. Null-coalescing, never truthiness.
Rejected — each will look appealing again
  • Hiding or reordering regions. There is one view. A dashboard the user assembles is a dashboard nobody can specify, and it is the path by which this becomes the product it was built against.
  • A range control on the year. The window is fixed at 52 weeks. A picker turns one honest shape into four that disagree, and each switch refetches a year.
  • Stacking a split day's clients in one cell. At 11px a stack is a smear. The majority takes the cell.
  • Top projects this week. With one to three clients, a ranked table ranks one thing.
  • Pie charts. A solo contractor's time splits two to four ways. A bar does that in less space and stays readable when one slice is 4%.
  • Team and comparison stats. There is no team. This is the slot Toggl wastes.
What this screen takes from the API
CallFeedsNote
GET /stats Unbilled, the month, Velocity Unbilled by client with aging, the month's cumulative series against its ray, trailing-quarter gross by client, and the billable ratio. One request, because they render together.
GET /calendar?granularity=day the year One call over a one-year range. Already groups by local day server-side, which is the DST-correct bucketing this needs and the reason not to build a second one. Returns { date, totalSeconds, byClient } and nothing else.
GET /clients?includeArchived the year's hues and legend Archived clients included, so a finished engagement keeps its colour instead of falling into the neutral band.

The year's cache key is its own, not a variant of the activity key: the range is fixed, so sharing one with a range the user picks would refetch 365 days on every change. It joins invalidateEntryData(), or the region goes stale on every timer stop.

The target lives on user_settings as monthly_target and monthly_target_unit, paired by a check constraint so neither can be set without the other — so the Settings field writes both in one patch, and clearing one clears both. Revenue's figure comes from month_revenue(), whose coalesce chain must stay identical to the other three — see data-model.md.

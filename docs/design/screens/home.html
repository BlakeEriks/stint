Stint · Screens

Home

The screen opened every working day. It answers one question at three widths — how much have I earned? — for today, this week and this month, and says how much of that is still waiting to be invoiced.

The frame — header, rail, dock, timer bar — is _shell.html. This page specifies the content column, which is the panel.

What earns a place
  • A region ships only if it carries a number the user cannot compute in their head, or a row they can click to act on. "Interesting" is not the bar.
  • Every figure on this screen is money the user has already earned, bucketed by when the work was done. Hours ride alongside as the raw material.
  • A figure is extrapolated only if it is monotonic. Earned accumulates: it climbs all month and settles, so carrying its trailing pace to month-end is arithmetic on a shape the series already has. Unbilled resets when an invoice goes out, so its projection would forecast the next invoice date and predict a drop to zero. The month's projection is the one forward-looking figure on the screen, and Earned is the only series that earns one.
  • Every region reads. An action on one is a link to the surface that owns the mutation.
  • The screen is the same on every open. One arrangement, one window per region, and the habit is built on finding the number where it was yesterday.
  • A figure rolls when its money changes. The count-up means "this number just moved", so it runs on arrival and on a value that actually changed. Returning to Home from another screen holding the same figures rolls nothing — a roll there would say it of numbers the user read a moment ago.

The panel at 2xl

Today takes a third of the top row and the week two thirds. Today's content is a figure and a few short rows; the week's chart needs the long axis for seven columns and their captions.

  • One row per task, with its summed duration. A task is a name under a project, so the same name under two projects is two rows, and the project follows the name in text-subtle. Rows sit newest first, by each task's latest entry. A task with a running entry reads as live.
  • The rows scroll inside the region past just under five, so a long day never pushes the panel down.
  • A day with no entries keeps its rows. Three of them, each an em-dash, so the column holds the height it will have once the day has work in it and the top row does not change shape at the first entry — the same reason an unworked day in the week's chart keeps its caption.
  • A placeholder row carries no pip. The hollow ring means internal work, and three of them would say the day held three untracked entries. They are also not a shimmer: nothing is loading by the time they render, and a pulse would promise rows that are not coming.
Three figure tiers

A figure's size is set by what the number means, not by which region it sits in.

TierSizeCarries
fig-hero 40px The month's Earned — the screen's subject, and the series the projection extrapolates.
fig-major 30px Today's and the week's Earned. One tier down, and equal to each other: the two short windows on the same question.
fig-minor 24px On track for, Unbilled. Two readings of the month's money that answer narrower questions than Earned does.

Two label voices, two jobs. The period header (region-head, 13px mono, sentence case) names the region. The figure label (figlabel, 11px mono, tracked caps) names a number. The header outranks the label directly beneath it because the two voices differ, and a date reads better unspaced.

The gap under a label is corrected per tier. A larger glyph sits deeper in its own line box, so a margin is set against the ink rather than the box. Each tier sets its own once, in the system block, never at a call site.

The week's bars
  • The bar's height is hours, so hours lead the caption at 15px and the weekday sits beneath at 11.5px. The number that matches what is drawn takes the weight.
  • Money rides the bar it describes, printed at that bar's own head. Inside the bar's column it cannot be misread as belonging to the day beside it, and the caption below stays two lines rather than three.
  • The scale is the week's own longest day, which fills 94% of the track. Heights stay a true ratio of hours; only what 100% means changes. Each bar prints its own money and the axis is unlabelled, so every column is read on its own terms.
  • A day with no work prints an em-dash and no bar. The caption keeps its hours line as a placeholder, which is what holds every weekday label on one baseline.
  • Today's weekday carries weight as well as colour. Colour alone puts the whole distinction on the one channel a colour-blind reader may not have, and today is the column the eye should find first.
  • The bars stand on a baseline. There is no "full" day for a ceiling to represent.
  • A bar is stacked by client, in each client's own hue, bottom-up in the order the legend names them. A day is rarely one client, and a single-hue bar would be the screen choosing one to stand for the rest. Internal work takes the same hollow treatment it takes everywhere: no hue, a neutral segment.
  • The stack is seconds, never money. The bar's height is hours, so its segments divide those hours — a segment sized by money would make an expensive hour taller than a cheap one and break the ratio the bar exists to show. The money at the bar's head stays the day's total.

Empty columns at the end of the week are the week not being over. That space is left alone.

The month

Three figures in a column beside a cumulative chart. A hairline separates the month's Earned from the two figures read from it.

FigureAnswers
Earned Was this a good month? Bucketed by when the work was done, so it never moves when an invoice is sent.
On track for Where does this month land? Trailing pace carried to the last working day. It moves when a long day is logged, which is the point.
Unbilled What is ready to bill? Work done and not yet invoiced. Climbs while you work and resets when you invoice. All-time, not the month — it is a balance rather than a period, and the backlog that matters most is the part older than September. It sits beside the month's figures without sharing their window.

Earned and Unbilled read the same for most of a monthly cycle and diverge the moment the invoice goes out. That is not a duplicate — it is the one cycle where two different questions share an answer. They are never summed, and neither is ever summed with awaiting payment (../principles.md). Awaiting has an invoice, a due date and someone who owes it; unbilled can still be discounted, written off or never billed. Their sum reads best and means least, and the honest version of what will land is awaiting on its own.

The lines under the figures

The three figures carry no sublabel. Each is a number with a name above it, and a line of qualifiers under one of them makes that figure read as the region's subject while the other two read as its footnotes. Awaiting is the column's one line, below all three.

  • Awaiting counts rather than describes, and renders only when there is something to count. A line naming the worst invoice says nothing about the others and silently drops them at two or more. One late invoice is the dock's subject, where it arrives with the action that answers it.
  • Unbilled's age belongs to the client rows, not to the figure. Age is what makes unbilled work worth acting on, and it is per client — a single worst-case age over a figure covering four of them names one and hides three.

What moves, and what turns green

Three events move this screen's money, and each moves a different thing. A shared motion would make them indistinguishable: the motion names the event before a word is read.

EventWhat movesColour
A timer stops Unbilled rises; the client's share takes the hours and its age resets. Neutral
An invoice is sent Unbilled falls by exactly what awaiting gains, and the open-invoice count rises. Neutral
An invoice is paid Awaiting falls and the count falls. success
  • Only the payment is green. Stopping a timer and raising an invoice are things the user did; a payment is the one outcome that happened to them. Spending the colour on all three spends it on nothing.
  • Sending moves money without changing the total. Nothing was earned — a boundary moved — and a figure that grew when an invoice was raised would be the screen inventing money. Earned does not move at all, because it buckets by when the work was done.
  • Never green on a figure falling. Awaiting falling is the same event as money arriving, and marking both makes the screen flash rather than point.

The climb

Earnings accumulated day by day, solid to today and dashed to where the trailing pace lands. The shape is the argument for the projection: a line that has been climbing makes its continuation believable. The line only ever goes up.

Four value gridlines and a week cadence. Axis labels are drawn in HTML outside the stretched viewBox, so preserveAspectRatio="none" cannot distort the type.

The strip and the legend

  • The strip beneath the plot is the month's client split, one band per client sized by that client's share of the month's money, drawn on the plot's own x-axis so it reads as a footing for the line rather than a second chart. It answers who the month came from, which the line cannot: two identical climbs, one from a single client and one from four, are different months to be in.
  • The strip is money, where the bars are seconds. The month's subject is Earned, so its split divides what was earned; the week's bars plot hours, so theirs divide hours. Each split matches the thing it sits under.
  • The legend names every hue on the screen, once, at the foot of the panel — the bars and the strip draw from one set of clients, and two keys for one palette is a second thing to keep in agreement. Internal work appears in it with its hollow ring, because a reader who sees the treatment needs to find out what it means.
  • The legend is the panel's footer. It sits below the same rule the month takes from the row above it, and the panel closes under it at the gap the rule leaves above it — bracketed evenly, so it reads as a band the card ends with rather than a row that floats after the month. Without the rule it reads as the strip's caption: a key for one region instead of for the whole panel.
  • A hue belongs to the client, never to the position. The same client is the same colour in the week's bars, the month's strip and the legend. Today's rows resolve a project's colour through useProjectColors(); the bars, the strip and the legend take useClients(), which carries the name each one also has to print. A palette that reorders between two regions of one panel is a palette that means nothing.
Constraints this screen inherits

CLAUDE.md owns the accent's two jobs, mono and tabular durations, colour belonging to the client, and 0 as a valid rate. What is particular to this screen:

  • The accent appears nowhere here. It is spent on the running timer in the bar below, so the screen takes none of it. success marks one thing: the endpoint of the projection.
  • Internal work takes a hollow ring in the entry list and the legend, which is what having no colour looks like on a screen that is otherwise keyed by client.
  • Every figure here is an aggregate, so each one is a rollup in SQL. resolve_entry_rate(uuid) is per-entry and summing hundreds of them one call at a time is the shape to avoid.
What this screen takes from the API
CallFeedsNote
GET /stats Every figure, the week's bars, the month's line earnedToday, unbilled, awaitingPayment, week (seven {date, seconds, amount}) and month (its earned, the cumulative series, the projection and the client split). One request, because the regions render together and a set that pops in piecemeal reads as broken.
GET /calendar?granularity=day each bar's split by client Its byClient is seconds per client per local day, which is what the stack divides. The bars take their height and their money from /stats.week; this call only says how each day's hours divide.
GET /clients?includeArchived hues and the legend Archived clients included, so a finished engagement keeps its colour instead of falling into the neutral band.

The month's client split is month.byClient, from revenue_by_client over the month — a rollup that answers any window, so the strip calls one rather than needing its own. Never resolve_entry_rate once per client.

earnedToday comes out of the month's own by-day series, which is already loaded and already grouped on the local date — a second query for one of its rows would be a second definition of the same amount. Unbilled filters on invoice_id IS NULL and is never called earned or revenue (packages/core/src/stats.ts).

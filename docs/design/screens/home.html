Home

The screen opened fifty times a day. It answers two questions a contractor cannot answer from memory: how much money is sitting unbilled, and am I on pace.

The frame — header, rail, dock, timer bar — is _shell.html. This page specifies the content column.

What earns a place

Toggl's dashboard answers how did I spend my time? For a solo contractor that is the wrong question — you already know, you were there.

  • A card ships only if it carries a number the user cannot compute in their head, or a row they can click to act on. Preferably both. "Interesting" is not the bar.
  • Stats are denominated in money. Hours are the raw material; dollars are what the user thinks in. Unbillable work has no rate by definition, so those rows show hours and an em-dash.
  • GET /stats is one call. The cards render together, and a set that pops in piecemeal reads as broken.
  • No card writes. Every card reads, and every action on one is a link to the surface that owns the mutation — a dashboard that edits data turns a stray click into a changed invoice.

Nothing outstanding: the card is absent, and the grid becomes one column
  • The aging figure is the insight. A total is a fact; a total with "oldest 22 days" is a prompt. Work aging past a month is the most common way a solo contractor loses money, and no competitor surfaces it because none of them know the rate.
  • Totals come from unbilled_by_client, grouped by (client, rate). One client can have work at several rates, and collapsing them misstates the money. It resolves through resolve_rate(), the same chain an invoice preview uses, so the two cannot disagree about the same work.
  • Each client row links to invoice generation for that client, pre-filled with the period covering its unbilled entries. That is what makes the card an action rather than a readout.
  • Unrated work contributes hours but no money, so the row carries its count and the amount is an em-dash, never $0.00 — otherwise the figure reads as low rather than as incomplete.
  • The five-row cap is server-side — /stats returns at most five plus a moreClients count. The total still covers every client.
  • The row is a grid, not a link wrapping buttons. The label is the link; an <a> containing a <button> is invalid and puts Tab inside the link.
  • The row sizes by container, not viewport, because the same card renders in this wide column and in the 280px dock.
  • Never labelled "earned" or "revenue". It is work done and not yet invoiced — money the user might still never see. Overstating it in a billing tool is the same trust failure as silently editing an entry.
  • awaitingPayment is never added to the total. Unbilled is work not yet invoiced; awaiting payment is money already asked for. Summing them double-counts the same hours. It is one line at the foot — a row per invoice would put ordinary, nothing-is-wrong invoices back on the home screen.
Pace

Month-to-date against a target, with the ahead/behind figure spelled out. "On pace" is derived from business days elapsed, not calendar days — a 120-hour target is six hours a business day, and reading "behind" on a Monday because the weekend passed is noise pretending to be signal. Holidays are not modelled, deliberately.

No target — the way in, and no bar
  • Only the delta takes the warning tone. Being behind on the 14th is ordinary; colouring the whole card would read as an alarm.
  • Billable ratio rides the business-day line. Contractors consistently underestimate how much unbillable admin they absorb, and one percentage is the entire finding — it does not deserve a card or a chart.
  • Revenue reports a figure but no delta. Hours accrue evenly enough that business days elapsed predicts them; revenue lands in steps as work is done at different rates, so "behind $2,400" on the 3rd would be arithmetic dressed as a finding.
  • Revenue is work DONE — invoiced plus unbilled at its resolved rate — bucketed by the entry's date, never the invoice's issue_date. Those are independent, so invoicing March's work in April would otherwise book March's money into April. A voided invoice releases its entries and stops counting.
  • The card renders with no target, carrying one line and the edit affordance. Hiding it made the feature invisible to the only account that had never set one. The empty state renders no bar — that is what "no empty progress bar" governs.
  • The pencil links to the field in Settings, which owns it. A dialog here would be a second editor, and a second place to look when the number is wrong.
  • The bar is capped at the target. A fill running past its own track reports nothing.
Activity

Hours per day, stacked by client. Ranges are 14 and 30 days; 30 is the default. A bar carries the number, which is what answers "was Tuesday a three-hour day or a nine-hour day?"

Gaps are weekends, and the tallest day sets the scale
  • Hue is the client, resolved as everywhere else, and never the accent — that belongs to the running timer. Internal work keeps a neutral that reads as worked rather than as rest.
  • Every day in the range gets a column, so a blank one is a weekend or a dry spell rather than missing data. A clean five-on-two-off rhythm versus a ragged one says something no total does.
  • The tallest day sets the scale, so the shape stays readable at any workload.
  • Clients rank over the whole window, not per day, so a band keeps its stacking position across columns. Order that jumps between days cannot be followed by eye.
  • Past five clients the remainder collapses into one neutral band. Those hours are still counted, so the column stays the day's real height.
  • Archived clients keep their colour. Work billed to a finished engagement belongs in the history, and dropping the hue would silently reassign those hours to the neutral band.
  • The range control is a real radio group, so one tab stop and the arrow keys come from the browser.
  • Only the ends are labelled; each column's own date is in its tooltip.
  • 90 days needs week bucketing server-side, for the same DST reason day bucketing already lives there. At day granularity it is a ~4px bar.
Customization

The Activity range control is the only customization that exists. Reordering and hiding cards are not built; if they ever are, they may not compose new metrics, choose chart types, or add a second copy of a card. "Pick your own dashboard" is the most natural path by which a restraint-first product becomes the thing it was built against.

The timer bar and the entry list are not cards and are not customizable.

Constraints this screen inherits
  • Warning is the alert channel, and Pace's delta is the only thing on this screen that uses it. Success cyan appears nowhere — nothing here is an outcome.
  • Nothing here is green. The accent is spent on the running timer in the bar below.
  • Durations and currency are mono and tabular, without exception. An unbilled column that reflows as digits change is the same defect as a timer that does.
  • Aggregate rate resolution belongs in SQL. resolve_entry_rate(uuid) is per-entry; the Unbilled card sums across hundreds of entries and must not call it N times. A set-returning rollup is the correct shape.
Rejected — each will look appealing again
  • Top projects this week. With one to three clients, a ranked table ranks one thing. If the distribution is worth showing at all it is a single horizontal stacked bar.
  • Pie charts. A solo contractor's time splits two to four ways. A bar does that in less space and stays readable when one slice is 4%.
  • Team and comparison stats. There is no team. This is the slot Toggl wastes.
What this screen takes from the API
CallFeedsNote
GET /stats every card Unbilled by client with aging, month-to-date against target, and the billable ratio. One request, because they render together.
GET /calendar?granularity=day Activity Already groups by local day server-side, which is the DST-correct bucketing this needs and the reason not to build a second one. Returns { date, totalSeconds, byClient } and nothing else.
GET /clients?includeArchived Activity's hues and legend Archived clients included, so a finished engagement keeps its colour instead of falling into the neutral band.

The target lives on user_settings as monthly_target and monthly_target_unit, paired by a check constraint so neither can be set without the other — so the Settings field writes both in one patch, and clearing one clears both. Revenue's figure comes from month_revenue(), whose coalesce chain must stay identical to the other three — see data-model.md.

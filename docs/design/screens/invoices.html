Invoices

The screen to open when money lands, which is why home needs only the one number.

Each screen — the list, one invoice, a new invoice — is one panel, and its sections are regions inside it: no background, border or shadow of their own, inset 18px, divided by a rule that stops 18px short of each edge. A new invoice runs its sections down one panel; an invoice runs its lines and its status. Rows inside a region are divided by the same rule, never boxed.

Charges that are not time

A fixed fee, a deposit, a retainer amount, a rebilled flight or licence. They sit in What to bill, beside the client and the period, because a charge decides what gets billed. Everything that answers "what goes on this invoice" is entered before the preview and clears it when changed — a charge entered after the preview would be a charge added to invalidate the thing it was added to.

Notes

Printed on the invoice.

Assigns a number and locks these entries. Voiding later keeps the number on record.
The whole screen: charges are entered with the client and the period; in the preview they land last, stating no quantity and no rate
  • A charge states no quantity and no rate. Its middle column is empty, on this screen and on the PDF, where the column is headed QTY rather than HOURS. 1 × $2,400.00 tells a client nothing the amount has not.
  • Charges follow the time lines, in the order entered. Time lines sort by description; a charge joining that sort would bury a $2,400 fee between two hourly rows.
  • A charge alone is a whole invoice. A deposit before any work exists is the ordinary case, so generation asks for lines, not for hours — an empty period with one charge still generates.
  • Adding or editing a charge clears the approved preview, the same as changing the client or the period. It changes what would be billed, which is the whole test.
  • A half-filled row is not a line. A row reaches the invoice once it has both a description and an amount above zero. Someone mid-thought is not billing zero, and the same filter runs before the preview and before generation — so what was approved is what gets created.

  • A draft is deleted; an issued invoice is voided. A draft holds no number, so deleting it costs nothing. Once issued the number is on record and only voiding is offered — that is what keeps numbering gapless. The UI shows one and never the other.
  • Downloading is the primary action, in every status. With no email, the download is how an invoice reaches a client.

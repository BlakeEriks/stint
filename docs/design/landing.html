Stint · Landing page specification

Track hours. Bill clients. Nothing else.

The marketing site for Stint — a time tracker and invoicing tool for solo contractors. This document is written in the app's own design system, so it doubles as the visual reference: every colour, type role and elevation below is the one the product already ships. Build the page from this and it cannot drift from the app it sells.

01

The strategy in four sentences

Every competitor's landing page is a feature list, because every competitor is selling a platform. Stint's page has to do the opposite: it sells what the product refuses to do, which means the page itself must be restrained or it contradicts its own pitch. A busy page selling simplicity is a broken argument.

  • Audience: solo contractors and freelancers who bill by the hour — one person, no team, no seats. Anyone looking for team features should be able to tell in five seconds that this is not for them, and leave. That is a success, not a loss.
  • The claim: it tracks your hours, knows your rates, and produces an invoice your client takes seriously. Free to use, fully.
  • The proof: the page is quiet, fast and beautiful, and the invoice is shown rather than described. Never claim it is well-designed — demonstrate it and let the reader draw the conclusion.
  • The competitor is never named. Describe the bloat ("project boards you never asked for", "team seats you don't need") and let the reader fill in the blank. Naming a company dates the page and invites a comparison the reader runs on someone else's terms.

Why the timer and nothing else

A screenshot of the whole app would compete with the headline and date the moment the UI moves. The running timer is the product's core sensation, it is the only element permitted the accent, and motion is the one thing a competitor's static hero cannot reproduce. It also sets an expectation the app then meets exactly, which is the cheapest trust you will ever buy.

Do

Tick live from page load, seeded at a plausible mid-session figure (1:47:32). Mono, tabular-nums, so no digit change reflows the layout.

Don't

Start at 0:00:00 — a two-digit number reads as unimpressive in the first frame, and the page's first impression is spent on nothing.

Motion rules

  • The dot pulses on a 2s ease-in-out loop. It is the only ambient motion on the page.
  • prefers-reduced-motion stops the pulse and the tick; the readout holds its seeded value. The page must be completely legible at rest.
  • No scroll-triggered reveals. Everything is visible on load — that first still frame is what a shared link and a search-result preview get.
  • Hero height is set by its content. Never 100vh.
03

Page order

Six sections. The order is an argument: hook, the thing only this app can tell you, the deliverable, where it runs, the price with its reason, and the ask.

01 Hero — timer, headline, CTA the one accent on the page
02 Know what you're owed unbilled money · the question you can't answer from memory
03 This is what your client gets the invoice, shown · one image, one caption
04 Everywhere you work web · mac menu bar · phone
05 Free to use, fully the price, the reason, and the three promises in one line
06 Closing CTA repeat of the hero button · neutral, not accent

No testimonials (there are none yet, and fake ones are unrecoverable), no logo wall, no feature grid, no FAQ accordion. If a section cannot be justified by one of the six jobs above, it does not ship — the same bar docs/design/principles.md applies to the app.

The copy, verbatim

Written to be shipped as-is. Sentences stay short; no adjective survives that the page itself could demonstrate instead.

Your hours, grouped by task, at the rate each one was worked. Your rates freeze onto the invoice the moment you generate it — re-download it a year later and it's the same document, to the cent.

One timer, one truth, on the web, your Mac's menu bar and your phone. The apps aren't shrunken copies of each other — each one does the thing only it can do.

Every feature, every number, every export. Nothing is held back, nothing expires, and there's no seat to upgrade because there's only ever one of you.

Later, a few dollars a month will take the Stint mark off your invoices. That's the only thing it buys — nothing that affects whether you get paid will ever sit behind it.

One timer, always. It never edits your hours behind your back. Your rates freeze onto the invoice the moment you generate it.

06 · Closing Two things, done properly.

Button: Start tracking — free · beneath: Free, no card, no trial countdown.

Words that are banned on this page

Never writeBecause
seamless, effortless, powerful, intuitiveEvery SaaS page claims these, so they carry no information. The page's own speed and quietness is the claim.
beautiful, pretty, well-designedIf it's true the reader can see it; if they can't see it, saying it won't help.
revolutionary, reimagined, next-generationIt's a timer and an invoice. Overclaiming here undermines the restraint pitch specifically.
earned, revenue (for unbilled)Unbilled work is money you might still never see. Same rule as the app — see docs/design/home.md.
Pro, Premium, UpgradeThe paid plan is one sentence about a watermark. Tier language turns it into the upsell the page is arguing against.
teams, collaborate, workspaceWrong audience. Attracting them costs you the positioning and gets you a churned signup.

Dark only, and that's a decision

The app is dark-first because the palette was derived for a dark ground, and in light mode the accent must drop 35 lightness points to #1F7E17 to carry contrast — it stops being neon and becomes signal green. The neon is the brand impression, so the landing page ships dark only, with no theme toggle. The app behind it still honours a light preference; the marketing page has one job and one look.

08

Layout and build

Layout

  • Single column, max-width 64rem, generously gutter'd. No sidebar, no sticky header.
  • No app nav. The landing page is not the app shell — no vertical rail. A minimal header with the wordmark and one text link ("Sign in") is all it gets.
  • Panels on bg-primary with shadow-card and a 10px radius, floating on bg-base. Never a card darker than the ground — it reads as a hole.
  • Sections separated by space, not by rules or alternating bands. Depth comes from shadow and radius, as it does in the app.
  • Every section is legible at 375px. The invoice image scrolls inside its own container rather than shrinking to illegibility.
  • Sections are full-bleed; Container holds the measure inside them, which is what lets a section carry bg-surface-recessed edge to edge.
  • Every grid needs an explicit grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)], including at the single-column breakpoint — a grid item defaults to min-width: auto, so the timer card's intrinsic width sets the column and the page scrolls sideways on a phone.
  • The <h1> carries an sr-only sentence covering both lists, and the visible ✗ list is aria-hidden. line-through is presentational and is not announced, so a screen reader has no other way to know the second list is negated.
  • The invoice preview is billed from "Your name here / you@yourdomain.com". Sample rows show the shape, and a name is not part of the shape — this is a public page that also renders bank-detail labels.

Build

  • A static route in the existing Next app — a real RSC page, which is what the architecture doc reserves RSC for. No second repo, no CMS.
  • Imports the generated token CSS by relative path, so a token change reaches the landing page automatically.
  • The ticking timer is the page's only client component: a dozen lines, seeded server-side so SSR and the client agree on the first frame — the same hydration trap useTimer already solves.
  • Self-host the two Plex faces; subset to Latin. No render-blocking font request to a third party.
  • Target: no layout shift, no third-party script, no analytics that needs a cookie banner. A consent dialog on a page selling restraint is an own goal.

Search

  • <title>: Stint — time tracking and invoicing for solo contractors. The H1 keeps its shorter voice; the title carries the search terms.
  • Meta description: Free time tracking and invoicing for solo contractors. One timer, your rates, and an invoice your client takes seriously. No teams, no seats, no upsell.
  • One h1, then h2 per section, matching the copy above.
  • OG image: the hero timer card on bg-base — static, with the accent readout. It is the one image that survives being 600px wide in a chat window.
09

Open questions

Not blockers for a build, but each one changes a detail above.

  • The wordmark. This spec assumes "Stint" set in Plex Sans 600 with tight tracking, no logotype. That is honest and quick; a real mark is worth doing once the page exists and is earning visits.
  • Domain and sign-in copy. "Email link, no password" is accurate to the PKCE magic-link flow, but the button may want to say what actually happens next.
  • The watermark's real price. The copy says "a few dollars a month" because the number isn't decided. Name it as soon as it is — a vague price reads as an undecided product.
  • Section 04 currently oversells. The macOS and mobile apps are not started per docs/architecture.md. Either ship the page without that section, or write it in the future tense honestly ("Mac menu bar and phone apps are coming"). Do not imply they exist. A page that lies about platform support is a trust failure on the same axis the whole product is built to defend.

Every rule here traces to docs/design/color.md, docs/design/typography.md or docs/design/principles.md. Where this document and those disagree, those win — and this one is wrong.

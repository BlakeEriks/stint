Stint · Screens

Home

The screen opened fifty times a day. It answers three questions a contractor cannot answer from memory: how much money is sitting unbilled, is anything about to go wrong, and am I on pace.

The frame — header, rail, dock, timer bar — is _shell.html. This page specifies the content column and the inbox that feeds from the same call.

What earns a place

Toggl's dashboard answers how did I spend my time? For a solo contractor that is the wrong question — you already know, you were there.

  • A card ships only if it carries a number the user cannot compute in their head, or a row they can click to act on. Preferably both. "Interesting" is not the bar, and decoration here is how this app becomes the one it was built against.
  • Stats are denominated in money. Hours are the raw material; dollars are what the user thinks in. The exception is unbillable work, which has no rate by definition — those rows show hours and an em-dash.
  • GET /stats is one call. The cards render together, and a set that pops in piecemeal reads as broken.

Unbilled and Pace share a row at lg on a 1.6fr / 1fr grid, not equal columns — Unbilled carries a list and Pace carries one figure and a bar, so equal columns would starve the side with something to say to pad the side without. Activity spans the full width beneath them, then today's entries.

The grid is conditional: both cards hide when they have nothing, and a fixed two-column track would leave a visible hole on an ordinary day.

No card carries the accent. On this screen the accent is spent, and it is spent on the running timer in the bar below. The progress bar is neutral.

Unbilled

The headline number, and the reason this screen exists. Sources time_entries where invoice_id is null, is_billable, and ended_at is not null — running timers never count, matching the invoicing rule that you cannot bill time still accruing.

  • The aging column is the insight. A total alone is a fact; a total with "oldest 22 days" is a prompt. Work aging past a month is the most common way a solo contractor loses money, and no competitor surfaces it because none of them know the rate.
  • Totals come from unbilled_by_client, grouped by (client, rate). The rate is part of the grouping key for the same reason it is on an invoice line: one client can have work at several rates, and collapsing them misstates the money. Its coalesce chain must stay identical to resolve_entry_rate, or this card and an invoice preview will disagree about the same work.
  • Each client row links to invoice generation for that client, pre-filled with the period covering its unbilled entries. That is what makes the card an action rather than a readout.
  • A client with unrated work carries its count on the row — "oldest 9d · 2 unrated". Those entries contribute hours but no money, so without it the amount reads as low rather than as incomplete. Unrated totals show an em-dash, never $0.00.
  • Rows cap at five clients with a "+N more" line, and the cap is server-side — /stats returns at most five plus a moreClients count. The total above still covers every client.
  • Never labelled "earned" or "revenue". It is work done and not yet invoiced — money the user might still never see. Overstating it in a billing tool is the same trust failure as silently editing an entry.
  • awaitingPayment is never added to the total. Unbilled is work not yet invoiced; awaiting payment is money already asked for. Summing them double-counts the same hours. It is one line at the foot — a row per invoice would put ordinary, nothing-is-wrong invoices back on the home screen.
Pace

Month-to-date against a target, with the ahead/behind figure spelled out. "On pace" is derived from business days elapsed, not calendar days — a 120-hour target is six hours a business day, and reading "behind" on a Monday because the weekend passed is noise pretending to be signal. Holidays are not modelled, deliberately.

  • The card hides entirely when no target is set. An empty progress bar asking to be configured is a chore the app assigned itself.
  • Billable ratio is the second line, not its own card. Contractors consistently underestimate how much unbillable admin they absorb, and one percentage is the entire finding — it does not deserve a chart.
  • Only hours render. A revenue target stores and validates, then the card says pace in that unit is not computed yet. Revenue means invoiced plus unbilled-at-resolved-rate, a different query from summing time entries. Saying so beats rendering hours under a money target.
Activity

Hours per day, stacked by client. Ranges are 14 and 30 days; 30 is the default. A bar carries the number, which is what answers "was Tuesday a three-hour day or a nine-hour day?"

  • Hue is the client, resolved as everywhere else, and never the accent — that belongs to the running timer. Internal work keeps a neutral that reads as worked rather than as rest.
  • Gaps stay real. Every day in the range gets a column, so a blank one is a weekend or a dry spell rather than missing data. A clean five-on-two-off rhythm versus a ragged one says something no total does.
  • Past five clients the remainder collapses into one neutral band — beyond that the legend becomes the card and the hues stop being separable.
  • 90 days is deliberately absent. At day granularity that is 90 bars in a ~660px card — a ~4px bar. It needs week bucketing server-side, for the same DST reason day bucketing already lives there.

It is always present, including when it is empty

A dock region is furniture — staying put is the entire point. "Nothing needs you." is information rather than noise, a section that vanishes leaves the user wondering where it went, and a region that appears and disappears reflows the page at the exact moment you fix something.

An inbox is a place, which is why it is named for one rather than for a predicate.

Ordering and tone

The runaway timer sorts first — the one row about time being recorded wrongly right now, where an overdue invoice is equally late in an hour, and the only row whose subject changes while you read it. Overdue invoices sort next and carry danger; the rest carry warning. Never the accent.

The grace periods are the point

An invoice fires at due date + 7 days, not the moment it passes. Net 30 with a client who pays on day 32 is ordinary, and a card that flags it trains the user to clear the list without reading it — which is how the one genuinely late invoice gets dismissed with the rest. The invoice page still shows the true due date; this governs only when the inbox speaks up.

What each row may do

  • The rows write, narrowly. Marking an invoice paid or sent is offered inline, because that is the action that legitimately clears a row — the underlying fact changed. Every write is explicit and names itself (Mark STINT-0014 paid), because a list of identical buttons is unusable with a screen reader.
  • The runaway row surfaces; it does not correct. Keep touches nothing and dismisses the notice — a long timer is often correct, and stopping it would be the app editing billable work. Adjust stops first, then opens the editor. Discard asks once. dismissed resets when the overrun ends, so Keep silences that overrun rather than the feature.
  • The unprojected row acts in place rather than navigating. These entries have no page of their own and scatter across days, so the today-only list would not reach them. It opens the editor on the oldest — the one closest to being invoiced without a rate.
  • Nothing is stored. Rows are derived per request, so marking an invoice paid clears its row because the predicate stops being true. An inbox table would need every write path to remember to clear it, and a stale row claiming money is late after it arrived is the same trust failure as silently editing hours.
  • Nothing destructive is offered here. Voiding and deleting belong on the invoice itself, where the whole document is in view — a stray click on a glance must not destroy a financial record.
  • The inbox cannot be hidden. Structural rather than a rule to enforce: it is not a card, so there is nothing to switch off. A region that surfaces problems is worthless if it can be turned off, and the user most likely to turn it off is the one it exists for.
Customization

The Activity range control is the only customization that exists. Reordering and hiding cards are not built; if they ever are, they may not compose new metrics, choose chart types, or add a second copy of a card. "Pick your own dashboard" is the most natural path by which a restraint-first product becomes the thing it was built against.

The timer bar, the inbox and the entry list are not cards and are not customizable.

Constraints this screen inherits
  • Warning and danger are the alert channel. timer-warning and danger carry the inbox's rows. Success cyan appears nowhere on this screen — nothing here is an outcome.
  • Nothing here is green, including the inbox's runaway row, which is about a timer and still renders in warning. Tested — test/ui/inbox.test.tsx asserts no accent class survives.
  • Durations and currency are mono and tabular, without exception. An unbilled column that reflows as digits change is the same defect as a timer that does.
  • Aggregate rate resolution belongs in SQL. resolve_entry_rate(uuid) is per-entry; the Unbilled card sums across hundreds of entries and must not call it N times. A set-returning rollup is the correct shape.
  • No card writes. Every card reads, and every action on one is a link to the surface that owns the mutation. The inbox is the narrow exception, with the limits stated above.
Rejected — each will look appealing again
  • Top projects this week. With one to three clients, a ranked table is a ranked table of one. If the distribution is worth showing at all it is a single horizontal stacked bar — one row, no header, no ranking.
  • Pie charts. A solo contractor's time splits two to four ways. A bar does that in less space and stays readable when one slice is 4%.
  • Streaks and gamification. Logging every day is not a virtue for someone billing hourly, and rewarding it pushes toward exactly the overwork that surfacing runaway timers exists to catch. The app should never congratulate a user for a fourteen-day streak.
  • Team and comparison stats. There is no team. This is the slot Toggl wastes and the inbox fills.
  • A snooze on the inbox. Hiding a row that is still true makes the inbox something dismissed reflexively rather than read. The grace periods make it quiet enough that nothing needs dismissing.

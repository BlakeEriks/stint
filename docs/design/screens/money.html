Money

The top region of Home, and the one the screen is named for. It answers what a contractor cannot answer from memory: how much has actually arrived, how much has been asked for, and how much work is still sitting unbilled.

The frame — header, rail, dock, timer bar — is _shell.html. This page specifies one region of the panel; home.html specifies the rest.

    Collected left, owed right — one subject in two halves
    • Two halves of one subject. Money that landed on the left, money that has not on the right. The panel pairs this way throughout. What keeps the figures from reading as a sum is that every one of them is named — no rule divides the halves, because a vertical rule rebuilds the gridlines the panel exists without.
    • Collected is the only figure at type-figure. It is the one number here that is finished — the money exists, it arrived, nothing can take it back. Awaiting and unbilled are claims of decreasing certainty and take type-amount-hero.
    • Awaiting and unbilled are equal weight. One is money asked for, the other money not yet asked for; the difference is a stage rather than a size.
    • Each carries a region's own head — an icon and a type-label — because each is one. The right half takes no title above them: a word naming the grouping costs a row to restate what the two figures already say.
    • An icon, never a pip. A pip is a colour standing for a client, and neither of these figures belongs to one.
    • The client list breaks down the unbilled half alone, and its heading says so. Both figures above it are money owed, and a list under both that totals only one of them reads as a breakdown of the pair. Unbilled sits on the right so the list falls directly beneath its own figure.
    • Every row links to generating an invoice for that client, which is what makes the list an action rather than a readout. The awaiting half is invoices, and invoices have a screen.
    • Four rows, then +N more. At five the right half outgrows the plot and the pairing stops reading as one subject.
    • A row whose work is not all rated carries N unrated beside its age, in the same type-meta. Unrated work is missing from the amount, so a row that says so is a figure the user can trust; one phrasing across the screen, because a figure marked one way in one region and another way in the next reads as two different facts.
    • Never a combined total. Awaiting has an invoice, a due date and someone who owes it; unbilled is work that can still be discounted, written off or never billed. Their sum presents the second with the authority of the first, and it is the figure that reads best and means least. The honest version of "what will land" is awaiting, already on screen.
    Two windows, because two questions

    The figure and the plot answer different questions, so they take different windows. Neither is configurable: the region has one view.

    ElementWindowBecause
    The figureTrailing 12 months An accumulator. It never reads zero, and a payment moves it up and leaves it there until a month rolls off.
    The plotLast 6 months The horizon a contractor can act on, and wide enough that one month is a legible share of the picture.
    The subtitleCalendar month Where a sometimes-zero figure is fine.
    • A calendar month is the wrong headline. Paid monthly, it reads $0.00 for most of every month and resets the morning after a cheque lands — a billing tool leading with zero while thousands are owed and last week's payment cleared.
    • A rolling window is the wrong headline too. It smooths the zero away and takes the arrival with it: a third added to a number nobody can name. Velocity can average because it reports a rate and says /mo gross; this region reports events.
    • last paid 14d ago is the cadence signal, and the reason the subtitle exists. Invoiced monthly, it is how a late cheque is noticed without arithmetic.

    The plot's scale

    The base sits below the lowest month by a third of the observed range, and the range is floored at 30% of the peak.

    Base atWhat breaks
    ZeroSteady income draws six near-identical points; the shape the plot exists to show is invisible.
    The minimumThe lowest month renders flat on the axis, so a light month reads as a month with no income.
    A fraction of the peakOne strong month raises the base above real months and they vanish from the plot.
    • The axis carries no values, because the scale is not proportional from zero. The plot carries shape; the figures carry amounts, and a labelled axis over a floored scale would be a lie about proportion.
    • The last point is hollow. The current month is still being collected, and a filled dot would claim it had settled.
    The beats

    Three events move this region, and each moves a different thing. A shared motion would make them indistinguishable — the motion is what names the event before a word is read.

    EventWhat movesColour
    A timer stops Unbilled rises; the client's row takes the hours and its age resets. Neutral
    An invoice is sent The client's row leaves the list, unbilled falls by exactly what awaiting gains, and the open-invoice count rises. Neutral
    An invoice is paid Collected rises, the plot's last point rises with it, awaiting falls, the count falls. success
    • Only the payment is green. Stopping a timer and raising an invoice are things the user did; a payment is the one outcome that happened to them. Spending the colour on all three spends it on nothing.
    • Send moves money without changing the total. Nothing was earned — a boundary moved — and a figure that grew when an invoice was raised would be the screen inventing money.
    • A row leaving the list is the send. The client whose work was just invoiced has no unbilled work left, so it drops out while the two figures exchange its amount.
    • The beat sits under the collected figure, on its own line. It reports the payment, and the payment is the left half's subject.
    • Never green on the open figures. Awaiting falling is the same event as collected rising, and marking both makes the region flash rather than point.
